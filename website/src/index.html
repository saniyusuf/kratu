Learn to read English from the language you already speak.

Kratu is a talking learning app for children aged six to ten. A guide speaks to the child in Hausa and teaches English letters, words, numbers, spelling and reading. Nothing to read, no password, and no signal needed once it is installed.

How a child learns

Hear it, say it, be heard, win it

A Kratu lesson is a conversation, not a page to read. The child speaks back, the tablet listens and decides, and the practice comes as games. All of it with the internet off.

Why it works

Built for a shared tablet, a child who cannot read yet, and no signal

Their language first

The language a child thinks in explains the language school is taught in. A question in their language, an answer in Nigerian English, every time.

Nothing to read

Every instruction is spoken aloud. A child who has never been to school can use it alone, from the very first screen.

It actually listens

The child answers out loud and the tablet judges it on the device, with the answer narrowed to the words on the screen.

No password, no typing

Logging in is looking at the guide and saying your name. The face is kept as a pattern of numbers, never a photo, and never leaves the tablet.

Zero bytes to run

Install it once from any connection and it never needs another. The AI models live on the tablet.

A miss comes back

Nothing goes red and nothing is failed. A word the child missed returns at the front of the next set of five, until they get it.

A miss is not a mark

"No, try again." In four languages, and never a red cross

Three wrong answers and the guide says it herself. After five she fills it in and moves on, so no child is ever stuck. What was missed comes back first in the next set.

Who it is for

For the child who has a language, but not this one

Every child Kratu is built for already speaks fluently, argues, jokes and bargains in the language of their home. What they do not have is the language every exam, form and job in the country is written in. They are not slow learners. They are being taught in a language nobody has taught them.

Who built it

In an ideal world, Kratu would not need to exist

A child should learn English at school. But the schools are not good enough, and the teachers are rarely better than the pupils: in Kaduna, two in three failed a Primary 4 exam. Until the schools can do their job, Kratu does this part of it.
